Linkup Privacy Policy

Learn what Linkup collects, how information is used and shared, and how long it is kept.

Last updated: 5 October 2026

Who provides Linkup

Linkup is listed publicly under DMT Entertainment. The official publisher is Maarten G. Dormenat Teysen. For privacy and support questions, email dmt2014.business@gmail.com. This monitored address also accepts account deletion requests.

Information we collect

When you sign in with Google, Linkup receives your Google account identifier and the email address, verification status, and name Google shares. We do not receive your Google password. Linkup also collects the display name and birthday you provide. Your birthday is private and is used to apply age rules. Linkup is for people aged 13 and older.

Google sign-in is being tested on Android. Apple sign-in is being prepared and is not currently available for Linkup registration.

Linkup stores the content and activity needed to provide the service: plans, asks, join requests, chat messages, friends, groups, blocks, and reports. If you enable push notifications, Linkup stores a device notification token.

Location and maps

The app uses your phone's location to center the map. Linkup uses the map area you view to request nearby plans; the map-centering location is not stored by Linkup.

Plans show the place selected by their host. For private plans, the public view shows an area of about 1 km, while the exact address is available to guests the host approves.

Asks use a rounded area of about 1 km and do not show who posted them. You can choose a place for an ask instead of using your current location.

Tonight Mode is off by default. If you enable it, your phone sends location to Linkup. Linkup stores an area of about 1 km, shares it only with the friends and groups you select, and removes the location within 15 minutes after the session ends.

Google Maps on Android receives the map area you view. Google's published information for its latest Android Maps SDK says it collects request and device metadata, the device IP address, crash metrics and stack traces, and a Maps-specific pseudonymous identifier. It may also collect map interactions such as panning and zooming. Google says it uses this information to maintain and improve its services.

On Android, place search and address lookup pass the search text or selected location to the phone's geocoder. The Android geocoder may use a network service. On a Google-backed device, that can involve Google.

Who can see information

Other Linkup users can see your display name and the plans, asks, and messages you share with them. Blocked people cannot see your protected content. Moderators may review reported content to handle safety reports.

Teens aged 13 to 17 have extra limits: they cannot see, join, or host adults-only plans, and cannot post or see Nightlife or After asks.

Analytics and errors

Linkup records basic usage events, such as when a plan is created, in its own database. While you are signed in, these events are linked to your account. When an error occurs, Linkup may store a short error message of up to 200 characters to help fix the issue.

Linkup filters some personal information from usage events. Short diagnostic error messages may still contain personal information from an error response. Linkup does not use third-party advertising trackers or ad networks and does not sell your data.

Services involved

Sign-in uses Linkup's own OAuth flow with Google. Linkup manages its own account and session records. Supabase hosts Linkup's database and server functions. On Android, Google Maps and the phone's geocoder provide maps and place lookup.

If you enable push notifications, Linkup stores an Expo push token and sends the notification token and notification content through Expo for delivery through Google Firebase Cloud Messaging on Android. Google's Firebase Android disclosure says the messaging SDK collects the app version and Firebase user agent, and its Firebase Installations component generates a per-installation identifier. Linkup does not use Firebase Authentication to manage accounts.

The Firebase messaging SDK can generate a registration token and installation identifier when the app starts, before Android notification permission is granted. Android notification permission controls whether notifications can be shown.

These services handle data under their own terms and privacy policies, including Google's use of Maps SDK information to maintain and improve Google services.

How long information is kept

Chat messages and their notifications are removed 30 days after a plan ends. Analytics events are removed after 13 months. Push delivery records are removed after 7 days. Expired or stopped Tonight Mode sessions are removed within 15 minutes after they end.

Other notifications, join requests, plans, and asks remain until you delete your account.

Safety report snapshots may remain after your account is deleted so moderators can handle reports and safety cases. These snapshots currently have no automatic expiry.

Your choices and account deletion

You can turn notifications and Tonight Mode on or off, block people, and delete your account from the You area in Linkup. You may also contact the publisher to ask for a copy or correction of your information. Email-assisted deletion is described on the account deletion page.

When an account is deleted, Linkup removes the account profile, linked sign-in records and sessions, plans, asks, messages, friends, groups, notifications, push tokens, and other account data. Plans hosted by the deleted account are removed for everyone. Safety report evidence may remain as described above.
